System Architecture Tier 1 & Tier 2

1. Two-Tier Proportioning Architecture

ConcreteMix AI implements a rigorous dual-engine design to separate statutory codal calculations from advanced composite research formulations:

Tier 1 — IS 10262:2019 Engine

100% deterministic mathematical execution of IS 10262:2019 and IS 456:2000 (Table 5) for conventional OPC, PPC, and PSC with mineral admixtures (Fly Ash, GGBS, Silica Fume). Computes exact water-cement curves, coarse aggregate fractions, and absolute volume yields.

Tier 2 — Literature-Anchored Engine

Literature-anchored reference proposal engine for 12 special concrete types (301 benchmark mixes). Uses multi-dimensional nearest-neighbor retrieval, absolute volume closure, and parameter clamping against published literature.

2. Why IS 10262 Does Not Apply to Special Concretes

A frequent misconception in civil engineering is attempting to force special concrete mixes through the empirical tables of IS 10262. Indian Standard IS 10262:2019 explicitly relies on empirical assumptions that break down for advanced materials:

Geopolymer Concrete: Involves alkali-activation (aluminosilicate polymerisation) rather than cement clinker hydration ($C_3S, C_2S$). The concept of a standard "water-cement ratio" does not govern compressive strength; instead, compressive strength is governed by alkaline liquid-to-binder ratio, $Na_2SiO_3 / NaOH$ mass ratio, and sodium hydroxide molarity (8–16M).
Ultra-High Performance Concrete (UHPC): Discards coarse aggregates entirely ($D_{max} \le 0.6$ mm) and operates at extreme water-binder ratios ($w/b \le 0.22$) with 15–30% silica fume and 2% micro steel fibers. IS 10262 aggregate proportioning curves cannot resolve this particle packing regime.
Engineered Cementitious Composites (ECC): Micromechanics-designed composite exhibiting 3–5% tensile ductility through steady-state crack bridging with PVA fibers and high fly ash/cement ratios (FA/C 1.5–2.5). Compressive strength is secondary to strain-hardening tensile capacity.
Statutory Notice: Special concrete results are served strictly as literature reference-based proposals carrying the mandatory notice: "Reference-based mix from published literature — not an IS 10262 calculation. Trial mixes and lab verification are mandatory."

3. Target Mean Compressive Strength (IS 10262 Cl. 4.2)

For conventional concrete, the target mean compressive strength at 28 days (\(f'_{ck}\)) is governed by:

f'ck = max( fck + 1.65 × s, fck + X )

Where s is the assumed standard deviation (3.5 for M10-M15, 4.0 for M20-M25, 5.0 for M30-M60) from Table 2, and X is the addition factor based on concrete grade (5.0, 5.5, 6.5, or 8.0 N/mm²).

4. Selection of Water-Cement Ratio and Durability Bounds

The initial w/c ratio is obtained from 28-day strength curves (IS 10262 Fig. 1) and checked against the maximum permissible durability limit in IS 456:2000 Table 5 for the specified exposure condition (Mild, Moderate, Severe, Very Severe, Extreme). The lower (stricter) value is adopted.

5. Absolute Volume Method & Physical Volume Closure

Every cubic metre (1.0 m³) of compacted concrete in both Tier 1 and Tier 2 strictly satisfies the physical absolute volume closure equation:

Σ (Mass_i / (SG_i × 1000)) + V_air = 1.000 m³ (±0.02 m³)

Standard component specific gravities (SG): Cement 3.15, Fly Ash 2.20, GGBS 2.90, Silica Fume 2.20, Sand 2.65, Coarse Aggregate 2.70, Water 1.00, Superplasticiser 1.08, Steel Fiber 7.85, PVA Fiber 1.30.

6. Rule Checks & Clamping Safeguards

Every output undergoes automated engineering checks before presentation:

  • Parameter Clamping: Input parameters outside published literature boundaries trigger an explicit warning (e.g. molarity > 16M or UHPC w/b > 0.22) and clamp to safe extrema.
  • SCC Rules: Powder content verified between 450–650 kg/m³ and coarse aggregate volume share between 28–35%.
  • Sea-Sand Durability: Strict chloride threshold check against IS 456 Table 7 ($0.6$ kg/m³ for plain concrete; zero permitted for prestressed/mild steel without FRP).
